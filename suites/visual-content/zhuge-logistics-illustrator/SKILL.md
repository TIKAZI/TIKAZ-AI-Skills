# Zhuge Logistics Illustrator

Designed, integrated, independently refactored, and continuously maintained by **TIKAZ**.

## TIKAZ Edition

- Type: original domain illustration workflow; no upstream images or generator-specific assets are bundled.
- TIKAZ contribution: logistics claim decomposition, diagram grammar, alignment rules, character-summary role, source ledger, and factual/visual QA.

## Workflow

1. Identify the logistics concept, audience, decision or learning goal, and claims needing source verification.
2. Select one diagram grammar: sequence, comparison, hub-and-spoke, layered system, risk checkpoint, or exception path.
3. Write a shot card with nodes, arrows, hierarchy, exact Chinese labels, character summary, palette, source status, and alt text.
4. Use a white 16:9 canvas, aligned peer nodes, restrained teal-green emphasis, and a small guide character only for the concluding explanation.
5. Generate or draw with an available tool, then verify labels and domain facts against supplied or authoritative sources.
6. Review at publishing size for reading order, alignment, edge meaning, contrast, cropping, factual accuracy, and accessible alternative text.

Do not turn an uncertain business claim into a definitive diagram. Mark assumptions and unresolved exceptions.
